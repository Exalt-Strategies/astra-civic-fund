The sophomore who turned a folding table into a chapter

She started with a clipboard outside the dining hall. A year later, her campus has an Astra chapter, a weekly phone bank, and a waiting list of volunteers.

Interview by Astra StoriesPhotographs by AstraNovember 4, 2026

Riya (fictional) Sophomore, chapter founder Ann Arbor, Michigan

The table is still there. It is the same folding table, the one with a wobbly leg and a strip of tape where the university's property sticker used to be, and on a Tuesday afternoon in October it is covered in voter registration forms, a laptop, and a bowl of mints that nobody takes.

Riya was nineteen when she set it up for the first time, alone, outside the dining hall. She says she registered four people that day. This fall, the chapter she founded has forty active volunteers, a phone bank that runs every Thursday night, and a group chat she describes as "permanently on fire." We sat down with her between classes.

Why a table? Why not just post online?

Because nobody had ever asked us in person. Every campaign that came to campus was looking for volunteers, not voters. I wanted to be the person who just asked, "Are you registered? Do you know where you vote?" Most people said no. Not because they did not care. Because nobody had ever made it simple.

What surprised you most?

How many international students' younger siblings and cousins were citizens and had no idea how any of it worked. Their parents could not vote, so it never came up at home. I was the first person to talk to them about a ballot.

Nobody had ever asked us. So we started asking each other.

Riya, chapter founder

When did it stop being a table and start being a chapter?

When I could not cover all the shifts myself. Somebody's roommate offered to take Wednesdays. Then a friend from the cultural association asked if we could do a table at Diwali night. That night we registered more people than the entire month before. That is when I realized the community already gathers. We just had to show up where it was.

How did you set it up properly?

Honestly, I did not know what a fiscal sponsor was until last spring. Astra matched us with an attorney who walked us through the university's rules on voter registration, what we could and could not say, and how to register as a student organization. It took two calls. I had been afraid of it for months.

What would you tell someone who wants to start one on their campus?

Start smaller than you think. One table, one afternoon. And ask for help earlier than you want to. The paperwork is not the hard part once someone shows you. Showing up every week is the hard part.

And after graduation?

I keep saying I want to work in policy. My mom keeps saying I should run for something. We will see who wins.
